Deep work is the ability to focus without distraction on a cognitively demanding task. Cal Newport coined the term. Most people know about it. Almost nobody does it consistently.

Here's why, and how to actually fix it.

## The Problem With Willpower-Based Focus

Trying to "just focus harder" doesn't work. Willpower depletes. Notifications win. Your environment is optimized for distraction by default.

The fix isn't discipline. It's design.

## Build a Ritual, Not a Routine

A routine is something you do. A ritual is something that *signals* your brain to shift state.

Before every focus session, do the same three things:
1. Close all tabs except what you need
2. Start a timer (25 minutes is enough to start)
3. Write down the one thing you're working on this session

That's it. Repeat it every time. After two weeks, your brain starts entering focus mode just from the trigger. I noticed this shift myself around day 10. It feels almost automatic after that.

## Why the Pomodoro Technique Works

The 25-minute work block isn't arbitrary. It's short enough that your brain doesn't resist starting, and long enough to actually get into flow.

The break isn't a reward. It's maintenance. Your prefrontal cortex needs recovery time. Skip breaks and your focus degrades fast. I've tested this more times than I'd like to admit.

## Tracking Changes Everything

There's something powerful about seeing your focus visualized. A heatmap of your sessions, like a GitHub contribution graph, turns abstract discipline into concrete evidence.

When you can *see* your streak, you don't want to break it. That's not a trick. That's how motivation actually works for most people.

## Start Small

Don't try to do four hours of deep work on day one. Do one 25-minute session. Then two. Build the identity of someone who focuses before you worry about volume.

The goal isn't a perfect day. It's a system you'll still be running six months from now.
